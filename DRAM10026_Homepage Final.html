01 — Welcome

This is a course for all of you

This is an especially rich and engaging subject and we're looking forward to some time together, talking theatre, plays, design and acting, and more.

For some of you, going to a live theatre event may be entirely new, while for others you are used to working in independent theatres, or you made theatre at school. It may even be the case that you are already an MTC subscriber. You might be making theatre in your spare time, working as a critic or are a super-fan of the abundance of activity in Melbourne's vibrant independent/fringe/underground theatre or performance scenes. This is a course for all of you.

It is not 'Rules of theatre', 'Encyclopaedia-Theatre, Australia' or a 'Theatre 1A' subject; instead, we want to turn your attention to the kinds of conversations that are happening inside Melbourne Theatre Company (MTC) itself: current discussions; programming of plays; and the dynamic contextual questions and provocations that drive one of the true gems of Australian theatre.

The subject combines Zoom tutorials with weekly module content on Canvas — including curated videos, recorded interviews, and resources that build your knowledge of theatre language and MTC's creative processes. Alongside these, you'll find practical guidance from a trained actor-tutor on the craft of speaking in public: how to structure an argument aloud, how to use your voice with confidence, and how to hold a room. These skills feed directly into your Interactive Oral Assessment (IOA), but they're also the kind of thing that stays with you well beyond this subject. Tutorials bring us together at key moments across the semester, and the IOA at the end is the one time you'll need to be there in person.

Throughout the subject we also consider the role and responsibilities of a contemporary theatre reviewer. How have theatre critics traditionally shaped the conversations happening around work for the stage, and how might they now? We'll think about our own position as audience-critics, examining the deeper social and philosophical threads that emerge from watching theatre and thinking about the relationship between theatre, theatre companies, and the urgent cultural conversations happening around us.

04 — Assessment

Three ways you'll be assessed

20%
MTC Quiz
Due Friday 27 March · Week 4 · Canvas
A multiple-answer quiz on theatre concepts and terminology from Modules 1–4. Completed online on Canvas.
30%
Research Journal
Due Friday 12 June · Examination period
Four 300-word reflective entries, each responding to a pair of curated videos. Written and/or illustrated. Fortnightly draft checkpoints are built into the semester timetable — each draft receives peer feedback through FeedbackFruits before you submit the final polished versions.
Your final mark will reflect the quality of your engagement with the draft cycle and your peers' feedback. Students who participate fully in the peer exchange process consistently produce stronger work — and the rubric recognises this.
50%
Interactive Oral Assessment
Week 12 · Wed 27 May, 5–7pm · In person · Founders Room, Southbank
A comparative review of both productions, conducted as a group discussion — 10 minutes of focused conversation per student. Supported by a 600-word written overview submitted before your session. All groups assessed on the same evening.
05 — Your Group

Choose your Interactive Oral Assessment group

At the start of semester you'll sign up for one of five IOA groups. This choice shapes your entire experience of the subject — it determines your FeedbackFruits peer group, the students you'll exchange journal drafts and video responses with, and the dedicated tutor who'll guide you through the semester. By the time you sit down for your IOA, you'll already know each other's thinking.
